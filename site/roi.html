How it's calculated

Hours saved per year= licenses × average hours saved per engineer per year.
Effort savings= hours saved per year × program years × loaded rate. Person-years = hours ÷ 2,080.
Rework avoided= 3 late findings caught while writing the analysis × 200 hours each × rate (10 to 30 times cheaper than at cert review).
Total value= effort savings + rework avoided.
License cost= licenses × price per month × 12 × program years.
Return= total value ÷ license cost, both taken over the whole program, so the two sides cover the same years.

Where the 57% comes from

Every ARP 4761A phase, with the hours a first draft takes by hand and the share the tool takes off. Aircraft-level and common cause work is counted once; system-level phases are counted for a reference program of 20 systems. An engineer working full time on these analyses gets 57% of 2,080 hours back, about 1,190 hours a year.

Where the savings come from

The iteration loop, not just first drafts

Safety assessment is iterative: every design change re-touches FHA, fault trees, CCA and traceability. The golden thread re-propagates and the engine re-quantifies instantly, so the expensive part gets cheap.

One connected model

Functions, hazards, fault trees, FMEA, common-cause, requirements and verification share one data model, so nothing is re-entered and nothing drifts.

Deterministic quantification

Minimal cut sets, BDD-exact P(top) and DAL allocation are computed, not drafted: the phases (PASA/PSSA/SSA/ASA) where manual effort and error risk are highest.

Automatic traceability

The trace web (function → FHA → fault tree → requirement → verification) is built and maintained for you, so change-impact is near-instant.

Fewer errors, less rework

A defect caught in-tool costs minutes; the same one caught in cert review costs weeks (the 1 : 10 : 100 rule).

Cert-ready output

Signature-ready FHA/PSSA/SSA and a DO-330 dossier in editable Word and PDF, assembled from the model, not hand-built.
